\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Cloud computing has become core infrastructure for public administrations and critical sections, but US is the main supplier of the most advanced cloud technology. The US CLOUD Act allows US authorities to demand data from American cloud providers, even when the data is stored outside the United States \parencite{doj2019cloudact}. For European governments handling sensitive data, this creates a conflict between technological capability and control. 

S3NS was created as a French answer to this problem. It is a company under
French law, fully owned by Thales, that offers a trusted cloud called PREMI3NS,
built on Google Cloud technology \parencite{s3ns2025premi3ns}. The alliance
combines two partners with different strengths: Thales brings French ownership,
operation and security expertise, while Google brings the technology. The
offer targets public bodies and companies that, under French rules, must keep
sensitive data in a specially qualified cloud \parencite{decretsren2026}. The
case therefore raises a central tension: S3NS promises sovereignty, yet depends
on a partner whose technology it cannot replace.

This report asks how the division of roles between Thales and Google shapes
ecosystem dependencies, lock-in, platform pricing, security incentives and value
capture in S3NS. Section~\ref{sec:task1} maps the business ecosystem around
S3NS. Section~\ref{sec:task2} examines network effects, path dependency and
lock-in. Section~\ref{sec:task3} maps PREMI3NS as a multisided platform and its
pricing. Section~\ref{sec:task4} analyses how security is provided and paid for
within the alliance, and Section~\ref{sec:task5} how value, costs and resources
are divided in its business model. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes. The
analysis uses the frameworks of \textcite{overby2021} and the course, and AI use
is documented in Appendices~A and~B.
