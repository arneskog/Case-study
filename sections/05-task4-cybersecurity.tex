\subsection{Task 4: Cybersecurity Economics}
\label{sec:task4}
\guide{About 900 words. Course basis: the cybersecurity economics guest
lecture. Identify the provision structure, map costs and losses per actor,
show at least one incentive asymmetry, and discuss correcting instruments.
Use qualitative levels or clearly labelled illustrative assumptions only.}

On a shared platform, security can be a complex but important part. This section will examine the cybersecurity economics of S3NS, with security treated as a jointly provided good. 

\subsubsection{Provision structure}

Efforts in security can be combined in three ways. Total effort is the sum
of individual security efforts, weakest link is set by the least protected
entity and best shot depends on the maximum effort \parencite[p.~1]{varian2004}. For PREMI3NS, the core infrastructure is
close to best shot. The software is Google´s and every
update gets analysed and validated by S3NS before it is deployed
\parencite{s3ns2025premi3ns}. Every client on the platform relies on the same core, meaning one provider sets the security level for all. This also mean that if the core fails, everyone fails. \parencite[slide 23]{kianpour2026slides}.

The identity and operations layer is considered closer to a weakest link. One compromised administrator account or a badly configured access policy can open the system to attack regardless of the strength of the rest of the platform. Shared identity and login systems are a typical weakest link setting \parencite[slide 21]{kianpour2026slides}.

Client organisations connected to a common platform are also a typical
weakest-link setting \parencite[slide 21]{kianpour2026slides}, and this
applies to PREMI3NS. Each client is responsible for the security of its own
tenant, which is its separate area on the platform. Vendors are even more
critical, because one application is used by many clients, so a badly secured
vendor can damage all of them. Client tenants and vendor applications are
therefore also weakest link.

To conclude, the strength of security on PREMI3NS varies. The core is well protected, but depends on one provider. Client systems
and vendor applications are where the weak spots are. With many different
parties involved, the weakest link is where a potential security breach is
most likely to happen. The structure of each layer is summarised in Table~\ref{tab:provision}.

\subsubsection{Incentive asymmetry}
\guide{At least one case where a partner's private optimum leaves the platform
under-secured, or where the loss falls on actors who make no investment
decision. Link the size of the loss to the network effects in
Section~\ref{sec:task2} and the subsidised side in Section~\ref{sec:task3}.}

Each actor values security by their own losses rather than the total loss caused by a breach. As a result actors will together spend less than they should on security \parencite[slide 15]{kianpour2026slides}. Vendors are on the subsidised side (Section~\ref{sec:task3}), and they are assumed price sensitive (A2). They can easily switch out PREMI3NS by another solution. Therefore, a breach on S3NS affect them less than it affect S3NS. Since their software exists in a weakest-link layer, and have the least to lose, they are likely to be the least-protected participant, setting the security for everyone \parencite[slide 21]{kianpour2026slides}.

PREMI3NS runs on Google´s software, but a security breach will harm Thales and S3NS. We assume they could in the instance of a breach loose both the SecNumClouds approval and their reputation. This is a weakness, as we know problems arise if the one who can protect a system is not the one who suffers if the security fails \parencite[p.~358]{anderson2001}. 

The ones who have the least impact in the security of the service carry large losses. The end user have limited control of their security, and client institutions dont have any more control than over their own settings. Two mechanisms discussed in section~\ref{sec:task2} make these losses larger. Firstly, all clients depend on the reputation of the same platform, so one breach will give them all collective hit. If we look at the scale of it, it will be reasonable to say that the more client institutions, the larger the loss in total will be. Secondly, since clients are locked in, they will not have the opportunity to easily leave after such an event. This means the alliance is less incentivized to invest in security, because loosing clients is a smaller risk, and the client institutions, who pay for the service, are also the ones carry large losses.


The imbalance can be written as an equation. Each actor $i$ has a security effort
effort $e_i$ that cost $c_i(e_i)$. The probability of a breach is $p$, and the actors loss in a case of a breach is $D_i$. \parencite[slide 15]{kianpour2026slides}. The equation to the left is the individual problem which every actor account for by themselves. To the right we have the equation which gives the best collective result. Every actors loss makes a part of the total, which could lead to actors spending less than what would be collectively best.

\begin{equation}
  \min_{e_i}\; c_i(e_i) + p\big(\min_j e_j\big)\,D_i
  \quad\text{vs.}\quad
  \min_{e_1,\dots,e_n}\; \sum_i c_i(e_i)
  + p\big(\min_j e_j\big)\Big(\sum_i D_i + D_{\text{society}}\Big)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Correcting instruments}
\guide{Liability clauses, SLAs with penalties, shared incident response, cyber
insurance, certification, regulation. Say which the alliance uses, and where
the public record is silent.}

To reduce the distance between the individual and collective optimal strategy one can use certain instruments that allocate others losses towards the one that controls the risk. The effectiveness of such an instrument is given by whether it changes behaviour of the actors before an actual breach happens \parencite[slide 26]{kianpour2026slides}.

PREMI3NS mainly has three such instruments. The first one is certification with PREMI3NS holding the SecNumCloud qualification awarded by ANSSI \parencite{s3ns2025premi3ns}. This mechanism facilitates that clients who do not have access to check the platform by themselves can trust the platforms security. This is a classic example of the answer to a market for lemons, but such an approval only reflects a snapshot of one point of time \parencite[slide 31]{kianpour2026slides}. Secondly, architecture is another instrument. The rule that only S3NS staff can operate the platform and the quarantine zone for updates \parencite{s3ns2025premi3ns} limit the channels where errors could spread. Thirdly, PREMI3NS is subject to regulation. Under the SREN law, particularly sensitive state data must be hosted by a service qualified by ANSSI or certified at an equivalent EU level \parencite{decretsren2026}. Raising the minimum level raises the overall security under the weakest link provision, and regulation is an instrument reaching the weakest participant \parencite[slide 30]{kianpour2026slides}.

All SLAs are with S3NS as operator, and Google Cloud's SLAs do not apply \parencite{s3nsdocs_keydiff}, so S3NS carries the contractual risk towards clients. We found no public information on how liability is split between Google and Thales, on security requirements for vendors, on shared incident response, or on cyber insurance. Such insurance would be a difficult product as a flaw in the shared code would affect all client institutions at the same time, a correlated risk which is generally difficult to insure \parencite[p.~612]{andersonmoore2006}. The main gaps in the alliance's security governance are therefore considered to be the vendor side and the liability split between the partners.
