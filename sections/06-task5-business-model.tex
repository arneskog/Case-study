A business model describes ``how an organization creates, delivers, captures, and keeps value'' \parencite[p.~283]{overby2021}. A Business Model Canvas (BMC) is a strategic tool used to visualize, design and analyse business models. It is divided into nine building blocks which are Key Partnerships, Key Activities, Key Resources, Value Proposition, Customer Relationships, Channels, Customer Segments, Cost Structure and Revenue Streams \parencite[pp.~285--287]{overby2021}. When analyzing an alliance, it is important that the alliance's model is kept apart from each partner's model \parencite[slide~31]{windekilde2026}. Figure~\ref{fig:bmc} gives a view of each building block and which partner is responsible for it. In this section we will analyze four of the building blocks.

\subsubsection{Block 1: Key Partnerships}
Partnerships are formed to gain the benefits of economies of scale, reduction of risk and uncertainty, or to acquire particular resources and activities \parencite[pp.~38--39]{osterwalder2010}. The last reason is an important one for the S3NS partnership with Google, the cloud software in particular. S3NS has been majority owned and controlled by Thales since it was launched in 2022 \parencite{thales2022, s3ns2025premi3ns}. Google holds a minority stake, estimated at between 5 and 20\% \parencite{alenatec2026, futurum2026}, but has no control over operations \parencite{s3ns2025premi3ns}. Google is mainly a partner through contracts, where they provide the cloud technology S3NS runs on. 

The partnership is a mix of competition and cooperation \parencite[p.~116]{overby2021}. Thales and Google work jointly on the platform, but customers can also choose Google's own public cloud. As a supplier, Google has strong bargaining power as the controller of the technology \parencite[p.~114]{overby2021}. Other key partnerships are SAP \parencite{thalessap2026}, software vendors, integrators and IT service companies \parencite{s3nsecosystem}. The conditions for the offering are set by ANSSI, but it is not considered a partner.

\subsubsection{Block 2: Key Resources}
Key resources can be physical, intellectual, human or financial \parencite[pp.~34--35]{osterwalder2010}. These can be spread across partners, where in PREMI3NS Google holds the core, its APIs and the cloud software. The SecNumCloud qualification depends on Thales and is held by S3NS. The physical resources are three data centres located in France. Each has a production zone, a quarantine zone and an administration system \parencite{paperjam2026}. Human resources operating and administrating PREMI3NS are S3NS employees within data centres in France \parencite{s3ns2025premi3ns}. As majority owner, the financial resources also come primarily from Thales.

The most valuable asset is the one S3NS does not own, the software. A qualified offer under French control is the scarcest asset, owned by Thales. A business model can keep value through lock-in \parencite[p.~283]{overby2021}. Lock-in on PREMI3NS runs through Google's APIs, so the client keeping asset is not considered to be controlled by Thales. 

\subsubsection{Block 3: Revenue Streams}
Revenue streams come from clients paying usage fees. PREMI3NS is priced at a premium of about 15-20\% above public Google Cloud \parencite{paperjam2026}. Contracts are structured without Google and signed under European law between S3NS and the customer \parencite{paperjam2026}. These contracts include the SLAs, which set the price and responsibility for the service \parencite[p.~247]{overby2021}, and all SLAs are with S3NS and not with Google \parencite{s3nsdocs_keydiff}. Therefore, all revenue enters S3NS while Google earns through license fees. But these terms are not public (A3). Since they are assumed to be subsidized, software vendors pay little or nothing. 

S3NS had a revenue of EUR 1.9 million in 2023, EUR 4.9 million in 2024 and EUR 13.7 million in 2025 \parencite{pappers2026}. The value capture is uneven with Thales as a majority owner will likely capture the most. 

\subsubsection{Block 4: Cost Structure}

S3NS sells a premium product and is therefore considered value driven rather than cost driven \parencite[p.~41]{osterwalder2010}. The cost of one unit in a value network is $c = m + A/n$, where $A$ is common costs, $n$ is the volume and $m$ is direct cost \parencite[pp.~110--111]{overby2021}. Dedicated data centers, employees in France, validation of updates in quarantine and audits to keep qualification make $A$ a large share for S3NS \parencite{paperjam2026,s3ns2025premi3ns}. As the Cloud platform is shared across Google's global customer base their development costs are shared. 

S3NS still serves a narrow client base, which means $n$ is still small and $A/n$ is high. Net result for S3NS was negative in 2024 and 2025 \parencite{pappers2026}, losses that are carried mainly by Thales as majority owner. Thales also carries most of the loss within the alliance in case of a breach on the platform. Thales is also assumed to fund the vendor subsidies (Section~\ref{sec:task3}). Both the costs and the risks of S3NS are on Thales, while growth pays Google. 

\subsubsection{Stakeholder Relationship Model}

The Stakeholder Relationship Model (SRM) shows the most important stakeholders in an organization's business model and how the interact. The relationships include exchanges of money and services, formal agreements and network effects, marked (+/+), ($-$/$-$) or (+/$-$) \parencite[pp.~288--289]{overby2021}. The SRM for PREMI3NS is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:srm}.

Money and control is primarily concentrated in S3NS. The (+/+) cross-side effect between clients and vendors is the main network effect. There is only a weak (+/+) bandwagon effect among clients, and a ($-$/$-$) effect from competition among vendors. Much of what makes the platform attractive to vendors comes from Google through compatible skills, APIs and tools, and not from S3NS or Thales. Jointly, the BMC and the SRM show that the distribution of fixed costs and risks is carried by Thales, while Google owns the technology and earns from volume.
